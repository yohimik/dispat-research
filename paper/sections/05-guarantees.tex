\section{The eight guarantees}
\label{sec:guarantees}

Throughout this section, \Head{} and the configuration are fixed and
$\st_1 \leq \st_2 \leq \cdots$ is a run sequence (\cref{def:seq}). Four
hypotheses are stated once and used by name.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.4em,style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[(H1) stable inherited-channel state] every baseline channel read by
    \code{Propagate-Channel: inherit} remains unchanged through the sequence.
  \item[(H2) no suppressed source is released] for every unit $u$ and every
    $p \in \scopef{u}$ whose contribution to $u$ is suppressed at $\st_1$,
    whether by cancellation, by a hold (\cref{def:sources}), or by a correction
    (\cref{rem:corrections}), $p$ is not released at \Head{} in the
    sequence.
  \item[(R) owed sources reach their target] for every $u$ and $d$ with
    $\admit{\st}{u}{d}$, every $P \in \owed{\st}{u}{d}$ reaches $d$
    (\cref{def:dist}), and $\dist{\owed{\st}{u}{d}}{d} =
    \dist{\srcp{\st}{u}}{d}$.
  \item[(K) propagation edges order and block] $\Kprop \subseteq \Korder$ and
    $\Kprop \subseteq \Kblock$.
\end{description}

The \emph{retry invariant} of \spec{13.7c} fixes, for every outstanding unit,
the corrected tuple stream, source sets, graph, scope and depth predicates,
target baselines, and resolved source/target channel admission. The only
permitted state changes are baseline tags written for packages that
successfully release. Thus it entails the constancies used below; (H1) and
(H2) name two historically problematic components, not a weaker substitute
for the full invariant. One consequence the invariant states in its own terms
is that $\deliv{}{}{}{}$ only grows (\G{5}); \cref{lem:deliv} derives it.

(H1) and (H2) are explicit parts of the invariant. They are named because the
1.0.0 proof audit isolated them in counterexamples (\cref{ob:origin,ob:g5});
they are not sufficient on their own. The fresh window of CCME 2.0.0 resolves
the prerelease-discharge defect of \cref{ob:pre}, while retries still require
every other input listed above to remain fixed. Every theorem below states when
it needs that full invariant.

(R) and (K) are not in \SPEC{}. (R) holds automatically for a unit with a
single source, since an admitted singleton is owed and reaches $d$ by
\cref{a:reach}, and for a target that has not released past $\cof{u}$, where
the owed set is the whole source set (\cref{lem:deliv-window}) so that only
the reaching clause is at issue. It can fail for a multi-source unit whose
delivered source reaches the target and whose owed source does not, and
\cref{rem:owed-reach} shows what that costs. (K) holds for the default
\Korder{} and fails for the default \Kblock{}, which excludes
\code{optionalDependencies} (\cref{def:graph}); \cref{rem:optional} shows what
that costs. Both are flagged for the specification.

\subsection{Lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[window antitonicity]
\label{lem:window}
Let $\st' = \Run{\st}{F}$ with $\wf{\st}$. For every $p \in \Pkgs$:
\[
  \Win{\st'}{p} =
  \begin{cases}
    \emptyset & \text{if $p$ published a non-prerelease version in the run,}\\
    \Win{\st}{p} & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
\]
and
\[
  \relc{\st'}{p} =
  \begin{cases}
    \relc{\st}{p} \cup \{\Head\} & \text{if } p \in \pubset{\st}{F},\\
    \relc{\st}{p} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
In particular $\Win{\st'}{p} \subseteq \Win{\st}{p}$, and along a run sequence
$\Win{\st_j}{p} \subseteq \Win{\st_k}{p}$ for $j \geq k$.
For the undelivered window, every successful release of $p$, stable or
prerelease, gives $\Winf{\st'}{p}=\emptyset$; otherwise it is unchanged.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{def:run} the run adds only tags of the form $(p, \vers{\st}{p},
\Head)$ for $p \in \pubset{\st}{F}$, which is the claim about $\relc{}{}$. If
$p$ is not among them, its tag set is unchanged, hence so are
$\stcommit{\st}{p}$ and its window. Suppose $p$ is among them and
$\vers{\st}{p}$ is not a prerelease. Then $\vers{\st}{p}$ exceeds
$\base{\st}{p}$ in SemVer precedence, and therefore exceeds
$\stbase{\st}{p}$: \spec{13.9} computes it as $\applyb{\stbase{\st}{p}}{b}$
for the graduation and stable cases, and the guards \diag{E185}, \diag{E195},
\diag{E153} and \diag{E156} reject any computed or pinned version that fails to
exceed the baseline. So the new tag is the highest-precedence non-prerelease
tag of $p$, giving $\stcommit{\st'}{p} = \Head$ and
$\Win{\st'}{p} = \Rch{\Head}\setminus\Rch{\Head} = \emptyset$. If
$\vers{\st}{p}$ is a prerelease, it is not a candidate for $\stbase{}$ at all,
so $\stcommit{}$ and the train window are unchanged. Its tag is nevertheless
the new baseline at \Head{}, so $\Winf{}$ is empty. The final claim follows by
induction along the sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The third case of the proof, a prerelease release leaving the window
untouched, is not an oversight in \cref{def:window} but its stated purpose
(\cref{rem:stable-window}). Admission nevertheless uses the empty undelivered window. \Cref{ob:pre} records the 1.0.0 defect that motivated this separation.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[delivery is monotone]
\label{lem:deliv}
Let $\st' = \Run{\st}{F}$ with $\wf{\st}$. For all packages $P, D$ and
commits $C \anc \Head$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.4em]
  \item $\deliv{\st}{P}{C}{D}$ implies $\deliv{\st'}{P}{C}{D}$; \label{dl:mono}
  \item if $\Head \notin \relc{\st'}{D}$ then $\deliv{\st'}{P}{C}{D}$ iff
    $\deliv{\st}{P}{C}{D}$; \label{dl:unrel}
  \item if $\Head \in \relc{\st'}{D}$ then $\deliv{\st'}{P}{C}{D}$ iff some
    $t \in \relc{\st'}{P}$ has $C \anc t$: iff $P$ has released $C$ at all.
    \label{dl:tagged}
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, under the retry invariant, $\owed{\st'}{u}{D} \subseteq
\owed{\st}{u}{D}$ for every unit $u$, with equality whenever $\Head \notin
\relc{\st'}{D}$; and along a run sequence $\deliv{\st_j}{P}{C}{D}$ is
non-decreasing in $j$ and $\owed{\st_j}{u}{D}$ is non-increasing.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\ref{dl:mono}: $\relc{\st}{P} \subseteq \relc{\st'}{P}$, and
$\basec{\st'}{D}$ is either $\basec{\st}{D}$ or \Head{}, which reaches
$\basec{\st}{D}$; so a witness $t$ for \st{} is one for $\st'$.
\ref{dl:unrel}: $D$ gained no tag, so $\basec{\st'}{D} = \basec{\st}{D} =: b$
with $b \neq \Head$, and every tag the run added is at \Head{}; $\Head \anc b$
would put \Head{} in $\Rch{b}$, so $b \anc \Head$ and acyclicity forbid it.
Hence no added tag is a witness and the witness set is unchanged. (If $D$ has
no baseline both sides are false.)
\ref{dl:tagged}: $\basec{\st'}{D} = \Head$ (\cref{def:state}) and every
release commit is an ancestor-or-self of \Head{}, so the second conjunct of
\cref{def:delivery} is vacuous. The consequences restate \ref{dl:mono} and
\ref{dl:unrel} over $\srcp{}{u}$, which the retry invariant fixes, and
induction along the sequence gives the last claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[outstanding-input stability]
\label{lem:source}
Assume the retry invariant. For every outstanding unit $u$ and unreleased
target $d$, its corrected tuple, source set, graph and scope/depth predicates,
target baseline, resolved source and target channels, and channel-eligibility
decision are the same in every retry.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the retry invariant specialised to one unit and target. H1 prevents an
inherited baseline channel from drifting; H2 prevents a successful source
release from changing discharge and hence the source set. The remaining
constancies are separate clauses of the invariant and cannot be derived from
H1 and H2 alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[admission restriction]
\label{lem:channel}
Under the retry invariant, admission of an outstanding unit $u$ at a target
$d$ can change only through \cref{a:window}, that is through
$\owed{\st}{u}{d}$, which is non-increasing along the sequence. It becomes
empty exactly when $d$ is tagged at \Head{} and every source of $u$ has
released $\cof{u}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All other clauses of \cref{def:admission} are fixed by the retry invariant
(\cref{lem:source}). The monotonicity is \cref{lem:deliv}; the
characterisation of emptiness is \cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:unrel} (an untagged
$d$ keeps its owed set) and \ref{dl:tagged}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[why H1 is part of the invariant]
\label{rem:origin-drift}
Without H1, the origin channel of an \code{inherit} unit can change when a
source baseline moves from a train to \stable{}. That can change an unreleased
dependent's resolved channel and planned version between retries.
\Cref{ob:origin} preserves the 1.0.0 counterexample; \SPEC{} prevents it by
fixing inherited baseline-channel state in the retry invariant.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[a planned package tagged at \Head]
\label{lem:tagged-target}
Let $\Head \in \relc{\st}{D}$ and $D \in \plan{\st}$. Then $\dirb{\st}{D} =
\bnone$, $\chanres{\st}{D} = \chanof{\base{\st}{D}}$, $D$ has no applicable
exact \code{Release-As}, and some unit $u$ has $\admit{\st}{u}{D}$. For every
such $u$ and every $P \in \owed{\st}{u}{D}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.4em]
  \item no release of $P$ reaches $\cof{u}$; in particular $\Head \notin
    \relc{\st}{P}$; \label{tt:norel}
  \item $\cof{u} \in \Winf{\st}{P}$; \label{tt:fresh}
  \item $\lnot\canc{\cof{u}}{P}$, $\lnot\held{P}$, $\dirb{\st}{P} \geq
    \bumpof{u} \neq \bnone$, hence $P \in \plan{\st}$; \label{tt:plan}
  \item if $\wf{\st}$, $P$ precedes $D$ in the publish order. \label{tt:order}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\basec{\st}{D} = \Head$ gives $\Winf{\st}{D} = \Win{\st}{D}\setminus\Rch{\Head}
= \emptyset$. \Cref{def:direct}, both candidate sets of \cref{def:channels}
and the exact-version disjunct of \cref{def:plan} all require a commit in
$\Winf{\st}{D}$, so the first three claims hold and $D \in \plan{\st}$ forces
$\propb{\st}{D} \neq \bnone$: some unit is admitted.
\ref{tt:norel}: by \cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:tagged}, $P$ owed means no $t \in
\relc{\st}{P}$ has $\cof{u} \anc t$; \Head{} reaches $\cof{u}$.
\ref{tt:fresh}: $\stcommit{\st}{P}$ and $\basec{\st}{P}$ are release commits
of $P$ when defined, so neither reaches $\cof{u}$, and $\cof{u} \anc \Head$;
by \cref{def:window} $\cof{u} \in \Winf{\st}{P}$.
\ref{tt:plan}: by \ref{tt:fresh} $\lnot\disch{\st}{P}{\cof{u}}$, so $P \in
\srcp{\st}{u}$ (\cref{def:sources}) forces $\lnot(\canc{\cof{u}}{P} \lor
\held{P})$. Then $P \in \srcp{\st}{u} \cap \scopef{u}$ and $\cof{u} \in
\Winf{\st}{P}$ put $\bumpof{u}$ into the join of \cref{def:direct}, and
$\bumpof{u} \neq \bnone$ by \cref{a:live}; an unheld package with a non-\bnone{}
bump is in the plan.
\ref{tt:order}: $P \in \plan{\st}$, $\basec{\st}{D} = \Head$ and
$\owes{\st}{P}{D}$; \cref{def:wf} orders $P$ before $D$.
\end{proof}

The lemma is the formal content of \spec{19.3}'s ``a dependent with no cause
of its own'': a package already tagged at \Head{} can be in the plan for one
reason only, an owed provider that is itself in the plan, untagged, and
ordered before it.

\subsection{G1: termination}

\begin{theorem}[\G{1}, termination]
\label{thm:g1}
Every traversal of \cref{def:reach} halts after at most $|\Pkgs|$ insertions
into \code{seen} and $O(|\Pkgs| + |\Edges|)$ work, regardless of the declared
depth, including $\infty$ (\code{+*}), and regardless of whether the graph
is acyclic. Propagation performs at most $2|\Units|$ such traversals, so it
halts, in $O(|\Units|\cdot(|\Pkgs|+|\Edges|))$ traversal work.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A package is inserted into \code{seen} only after a test that it is absent, and
\code{seen} is never cleared, so there are at most $|\Pkgs|$ insertions. Only
inserted packages (and the initial sources, inserted at seeding) ever enter
\code{next} and hence \code{frontier}, so each package is expanded at most
once, and the edges scanned across the whole traversal are therefore at most
$\sum_{p}|\Dep{p}| = |\Edges|$. The loop terminates either by exhausting
$\ell$ iterations or, when $\ell = \infty$, by the empty-frontier break, which
must occur within $|\Pkgs|$ iterations since each non-empty frontier consumes
at least one distinct unvisited package. No step of this argument uses
acyclicity: a cycle merely means some edge leads to a package already in
\code{seen}, and that edge is skipped. The outer loops of \spec{9.2} run over
the finite set \Units{} once per axis; the per-target work of phase 3 (the
owed set and the resolvability test) is finite and adds no traversal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Acyclicity (\cref{def:graph}) is therefore not what makes propagation
terminate; \code{seen} is. Acyclicity is needed for the publish order
(\cref{prop:order}), not here. This matters because it means an implementation
that loosens the \diag{E200} check does not silently gain a non-terminating
traversal; it gains an unorderable publish phase, which is a different and
louder failure.
\end{remark}

\subsection{G2: completeness}

\begin{theorem}[\G{2}, completeness: no orphans]
\label{thm:g2}
Assume the retry invariant. If $\admit{\st_1}{u}{D}$, then for every $j$
either $\owed{\st_j}{u}{D} = \emptyset$ or $\admit{\st_j}{u}{D}$, and in the
latter case $\propb{\st_j}{D} \geq \pbump{u}$. Moreover $\owed{\st_j}{u}{D} =
\emptyset$ holds only if $D$ is tagged at \Head{} in $\st_j$ and every $P \in
\srcp{\st}{u}$ has a release reaching $\cof{u}$. A release by a source alone
does not discharge the contribution, and neither does a release by $D$ at a
commit reaching no release of some source carrying $\cof{u}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take the conjuncts of \cref{def:admission} in turn. \Cref{a:live} depends only
on the unit. \Cref{a:scope} and \cref{a:cancel} depend only on \Head{} and the
configuration. \Cref{a:reach} is $D \in \reachset{\srcp{\st_j}{u}}{\pdepth{u}}$,
and by the retry invariant the source set is unchanged, as are the graph and
the depth, so the traversal is literally the same computation.
\Cref{a:resolvable} is constant by \cref{lem:source}. \Cref{a:window} is the
one clause that varies, and it is exactly the disjunction in the statement. So
whenever $\owed{\st_j}{u}{D} \neq \emptyset$ every conjunct holds, and
$\propb{\st_j}{D}$, a join over the admitted units (\cref{def:propagated}) with
$u$ among them, is at least $\pbump{u}$.

For the ``moreover'': $\owed{\st_1}{u}{D} \neq \emptyset$ by
\cref{a:window} at $\st_1$, so some $P \in \srcp{\st}{u}$ has gone from
undelivered to delivered between $\st_1$ and $\st_j$. If $\Head \notin
\relc{\st_j}{D}$, \cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:unrel} applied along the sequence
says nothing changed; so $D$ is tagged at \Head{} in $\st_j$, and then
\cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:tagged} makes $\owed{\st_j}{u}{D} = \emptyset$
equivalent to every source of $u$ having a release reaching $\cof{u}$. The two
final sentences are the contrapositive read against the same lemma: a source's
release leaves $\Head \notin \relc{}{D}$ untouched, and a release of $D$ at
\Head{} with some source still lacking a release reaching $\cof{u}$ leaves that
source owed.
\end{proof}

The theorem is the formal content of ``there are no orphans'', and it now
covers both shapes of \spec{13.7a}. A provider that publishes while a
consumer's publish fails leaves the consumer's owed set intact
(\cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:unrel}), so the consumer is planned again until it
releases. A consumer that proceeds past the commit on a cause of its own while
its provider fails is tagged at \Head{} with the provider unreleased; by
\cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:tagged} the provider is still owed, and the consumer is
planned again, ordered after the provider (\cref{lem:tagged-target}), until
both have released. Note where the proof would break under the readings of
\cref{a:window} that \cref{ex:orphan} contrasts. Against the source's window,
the provider's successful release would empty \emph{that} window, the unit
would cease to be a propagation source, and the waiting consumer would never
be released. Against the target's window, the proceeding consumer's own
release would empty \emph{that} window, and the consumer that got ahead would
never be planned again. One membership test, evaluated against the right
relation, is the whole difference between a resumable release and a silently
lossy one.

\begin{remark}[the discharge that is not a delivery]
\label{rem:g2-loss}
The ``moreover'' clause is satisfied, and the contribution leaves the plan, in
one situation where nothing was delivered: $D$ already tagged at \Head{} when
the sequence starts, its owed provider $P$ publishing at \Head{} in a run in
which $D$ then fails. \Cref{prop:loss} describes it; the run reports
\diag{E201} and the theorem does not claim otherwise. Where $D$ is not tagged
at \Head{} in $\st_1$ the situation cannot arise, since then $\Head \notin
\relc{\st_j}{D}$ until $D$ itself releases in the sequence.
\end{remark}

\subsection{G3: version stability}

\begin{theorem}[\G{3}, version stability]
\label{thm:g3}
Assume the retry invariant. If $d \in \plan{\st_1}$, $d$ is not released in
runs $1..k-1$, and no run $i < k$ reports \diag{E201} for $d$
(\cref{def:run}), then $d \in \plan{\st_k}$ and $\vers{\st_k}{d} =
\vers{\st_1}{d}$. The third hypothesis is vacuous when $\Head \notin
\relc{\st_1}{d}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First, $\owed{\st_k}{u}{d} = \owed{\st_1}{u}{d}$ for every unit $u$. If
$\Head \notin \relc{\st_1}{d}$ then, $d$ having gained no tag, $\Head \notin
\relc{\st_k}{d}$ and \cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:unrel} gives equality run by
run; this is also why the third hypothesis is then vacuous, since reporting
\diag{E201} for $d$ requires $\Head \in \relc{\st_i}{d}$. If $\Head \in
\relc{\st_1}{d}$, then $\basec{\st_i}{d} = \Head$ throughout, and by
\cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:tagged} the owed set can shrink only when some $P \in
\owed{\st_i}{u}{d}$ receives a tag at \Head{} in run $i < k$, that is $P \in
\pubset{\st_i}{F_i}$. Since $\owed{\st_i}{u}{d} \neq \emptyset$ and the other
clauses are constant, $\admit{\st_i}{u}{d}$, so $\owes{\st_i}{P}{d}$. Run $i$
published something, so it was not refused and $\wf{\st_i}$ holds
(\cref{def:run}); hence $d \in \plan{\st_i}$ after $P$. $d$ was
not released in run $i$, and it was not blocked, because $P \notin X_d$ gives
it a cause of its own (\cref{def:owncause}); so $d \in F_i$, and run $i$
reports \diag{E201} for $d$, which the hypothesis excludes.

Now $d$ gained no tag, so $\base{\st_k}{d} = \base{\st_1}{d}$ and
$\stbase{\st_k}{d} = \stbase{\st_1}{d}$, and by \cref{lem:window} its windows
are unchanged. Then $\dirb{\st_k}{d} = \dirb{\st_1}{d}$, because
\cref{def:direct} reads the window, $\scopef{u}$, and $\srcp{\st}{u}$, the last
constant by the retry invariant. And $\propb{\st_k}{d} = \propb{\st_1}{d}$:
every clause of \cref{def:admission} is constant for every unit, \cref{a:window}
by the first paragraph and the rest by \cref{lem:source}. Hence $\effb{\st_k}{d} = \effb{\st_1}{d}$. Its resolved and baseline channels, changed-provider status, and applicable exact \code{Release-As} cause are also fixed by the retry invariant. Thus whichever disjunct of \cref{def:plan} put $d$ in the initial plan still holds. For a fixed version function, \spec{13.9} reads unchanged stable and current baselines, resolved channel, applicable exact version, and corrected train records. Those inputs are fixed for an unreleased target by the retry invariant, so its result is unchanged. This proves replay stability. The graduation core itself follows the specific train-target rule of \spec{11.5}; \cref{ob:graduation} records the inconsistent \spec{13.9} summary rather than treating that intent as absent.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the prerelease case survives, for a different reason]
\label{rem:g3-pre}
For a target $d$ planned on a prerelease channel, the version is
$\coreof{\cdot}$-plus-counter per \spec{11.4}, and the counter is read from
$\base{\st}{d}$, which is unchanged precisely because $d$ was \emph{not}
released. This preserves the counter, but extending \cref{thm:g3} to
prerelease targets still requires invariant resolved channels and admitted
contributions, including H1's channel-stability component. The
prerelease problem of \cref{ob:pre} is about packages that \emph{were}
released, and is a convergence failure, not a stability failure.
\end{remark}

\Cref{thm:g3} is as much an operational property as a formal one: after a
partial failure, the re-run publishes the same numbers the operator already
reviewed. It is what makes a resumed release a continuation rather than a fresh
decision. It says nothing about a package that \emph{was} released: a
consumer that proceeded is planned again at a patch on its new baseline
(\code{cli@2.1.1} in \cref{ex:orphan}), which is the version its catch-up is
owed, not the one it was first planned at.

\code{api} keep their numbers. \Cref{thm:g4}: after run 2 nothing is owed.
\Cref{thm:g6}: run 1 published a package and the plan did not shrink; what it
did was tag \code{cli} at \Head{} for the first time, which is the measure the
proof uses.}
\label{tab:ahead}
\end{table}

\subsection{G4: no double release}

\begin{theorem}[\G{4}, no double ledger delivery]
\label{thm:g4}
Assume the retry invariant. If $D$ is released in run $k$ and, after run
$k$, every $P \in \srcp{\st}{u}$ has a release reaching $\cof{u}$, then
$\admit{\st_j}{u}{D}$ is false for every $j>k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Head \in \relc{\st_{k+1}}{D}$, so by \cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:tagged} every
source of $u$ has delivered $\cof{u}$ to $D$ and $\owed{\st_{k+1}}{u}{D} =
\emptyset$. The owed set is non-increasing (\cref{lem:deliv}), so it stays
empty and \cref{a:window} fails at every later $\st_j$. The full train window
may retain $\cof{u}$ for aggregate prerelease version arithmetic; it cannot
re-admit the contribution.
\end{proof}

A $D$ that proceeded past $\cof{u}$ before a source released it does not meet
the hypothesis: that source still lacks a release reaching $\cof{u}$, $D$ is
admitted again (\cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:tagged}), and it is bumped once more
when the release comes. That later release is the delivery, not a second one.
The sufficient condition of CCME 2.0.0, ``released at a commit whose ancestry
contains $\cof{u}$'', is no longer sufficient on its own, and \cref{ex:orphan}
shows why it must not be.

\begin{corollary}[exactly-once ledger discharge]
\label{cor:exactly-once}
Under the retry invariant, a contribution admitted to $D$ remains admitted
until $D$ is tagged at \Head{} with every source of $u$ having released
$\cof{u}$ (\cref{thm:g2}), and is never re-admitted after that
(\cref{thm:g4}). If the sequence delivers it, it is therefore discharged in the
tag ledger exactly once. $D$ may be published more than once meanwhile, a
consumer that proceeded being the case, but the discharging release is the
last of those. The single exception is the reported loss of \cref{prop:loss},
where the ledger records a discharge that no release of $D$ performed.
\end{corollary}

This is the idempotent-consumer property of the saga, obtained without a
deduplication table: the git tags are the record, and delivery is the query
against them. This is not an exactly-once guarantee for external side effects.
A publish can succeed before its tag is recorded; recovery across that gap
requires the verified adoption of \spec{19.4} or an idempotent publisher.
The ledger proof alone cannot prevent a repeated external operation.

\subsection{G5: no blast-radius widening}

\begin{theorem}[\G{5}, no blast-radius widening]
\label{thm:g5}
Assume the retry invariant. For every unit $u$ and every $j \geq 1$,
\[
  \{\, d : \admit{\st_j}{u}{d} \,\} \;\subseteq\; \{\, d : \admit{\st_1}{u}{d} \,\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compare the conjuncts of \cref{def:admission}, as in \cref{thm:g2}, but now in
the direction of restriction. \Cref{a:live,a:scope,a:cancel} are constant.
\Cref{a:reach} is constant by the retry invariant: the traversal has
the same source set, the same depth and the same graph, all fixed at \Head{}.
\Cref{a:resolvable} is constant under the retry invariant. \Cref{a:window},
$\owed{\st_j}{u}{d} \neq \emptyset$, is antitone in $j$ by \cref{lem:deliv}:
$\deliv{}{}{}{}$ only grows. A conjunction of constant and antitone predicates
is antitone.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[why this is a security property]
\spec{18.1} depends on \cref{thm:g5}. If the admitted set could grow between
runs, an attacker able to induce a publish failure (by exhausting a
registry quota, say) could enlarge the set of packages a commit releases
\emph{after} that commit had been reviewed and merged. The theorem says the
achievable effect of inducing a failure is confined to delaying a subset of
what was already going to be released. Delivery preserves this: a target it
admits that the window test would not is one that released past $\cof{u}$ on
its own account while within the unit's reach, and the unit's traversal,
scope and depth are what they were, so nothing outside the unit's declared
blast radius is reached.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[the plan is non-increasing]
\label{lem:plan-mono}
Assume the retry invariant. Then $\plan{\st_{j+1}} \subseteq \plan{\st_j}$ for
every $j$, hence $\plan{\st_j} \subseteq \plan{\st_1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $p \in \plan{\st_{j+1}}$; $\held{p}$ is constant, so $p$ is not held at
$\st_j$ either. If $p$ was released in run $j$, then $\Winf{\st_{j+1}}{p} =
\emptyset$ (\cref{lem:window}) and by \cref{lem:tagged-target} its only cause
is a unit $u$ with $\admit{\st_{j+1}}{u}{p}$; by \cref{thm:g5}
$\admit{\st_j}{u}{p}$, so $\propb{\st_j}{p} \neq \bnone$ and $p \in
\plan{\st_j}$. If $p$ was not released, its windows and tags are unchanged, so
$\dirb{\st}{p}$ and the exact-version disjunct are unchanged,
$\propb{\st}{p}$ can only have shrunk (\cref{thm:g5} applied to each unit),
and $\chanres{\st}{p}$ and $\chanof{\base{\st}{p}}$ are unchanged under the
retry invariant; so every disjunct of \cref{def:plan} that holds at
$\st_{j+1}$ held at $\st_j$.
\end{proof}

What the lemma no longer gives is strict decrease. A package released in run
$j$ can be in $\plan{\st_{j+1}}$: it proceeded past a commit its provider has
not delivered. \Cref{thm:g6} therefore counts something else.

\begin{lemma}[well-formedness is preserved]
\label{lem:wf}
Assume the retry invariant, (R), and $\Kprop \subseteq \Korder$. If
$\wf{\st_j}$ then $\wf{\st_{j+1}}$. Hence every state of a run sequence is
well-formed and no run of it is refused.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $P \in \plan{\st_{j+1}}$, $\basec{\st_{j+1}}{D} = \Head$ and
$\owes{\st_{j+1}}{P}{D}$ through a unit $u$. By \cref{thm:g5}
$\admit{\st_j}{u}{D}$, and by \cref{lem:deliv} $P \in \owed{\st_j}{u}{D}$, so
$P \in \plan{\st_j}$ (\cref{lem:plan-mono}) and $\owes{\st_j}{P}{D}$. Since
$\owed{\st_{j+1}}{u}{D} \neq \emptyset$ and $\held{D}$ is constant, $D \in
\plan{\st_{j+1}}$ once $D \in \plan{\st_j}$ or $\propb{\st_{j+1}}{D} \neq
\bnone$; the latter holds outright. For the order: by (R) $P$ reaches $D$ over
\Kprop{}, hence over \Korder{}, and the topological order of \cref{def:order}
places $P$ before $D$. The second sentence is induction from $\wf{\st_1}$
(\cref{def:seq}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{G6: convergence}

Say that run $j$ \emph{makes progress} when it publishes a package $p$ with
$\Head \notin \relc{\st_j}{p}$: a package tagged at \Head{} for the first time.

\begin{theorem}[\G{6}, convergence]
\label{thm:g6}
Assume the retry invariant, and suppose every run whose plan is non-empty
makes progress. Let $n = |\plan{\st_1}|$. Then $\plan{\st_{n+1}} = \emptyset$.
\end{theorem}

A refused run (\cref{def:run}) publishes nothing, so the hypothesis entails
that no run with a non-empty plan is refused; \cref{lem:wf} says when that is
automatic.

\begin{proof}
First, $\plan{\st_{j+1}} \subseteq \plan{\st_j}$, so $\plan{\st_j} \subseteq
\plan{\st_1}$ for every $j$ (\cref{lem:plan-mono}).

Let $\mu_j = |\{\,p \in \plan{\st_1} : \Head \notin \relc{\st_j}{p}\,\}|$, the
planned packages not yet tagged at \Head{}. Tags only accumulate, so $\mu$ is
non-increasing, and $\mu_1 \leq n$. If run $j$ makes progress, the package
it tags for the first time lies in $\plan{\st_j} \subseteq \plan{\st_1}$, so
$\mu_{j+1} < \mu_j$. And if $\plan{\st_j} \neq \emptyset$ then $\mu_j \geq 1$:
take $p \in \plan{\st_j}$; either $\Head \notin \relc{\st_j}{p}$, or by
\cref{lem:tagged-target} there is $P \in \plan{\st_j} \subseteq \plan{\st_1}$
with $\Head \notin \relc{\st_j}{P}$. So while the plan is non-empty each run
strictly decreases a quantity that is at least $1$ and at most $n$; the plan is
therefore empty by run $n+1$, and once empty it stays empty by the first
paragraph.
\end{proof}

The old measure, $|\plan{\st_j}|$, fails here: in \cref{tab:ahead} run 1
publishes \code{cli} and $|\plan{\st_2}| = |\plan{\st_1}|$. The bound $n$
survives because every package is tagged at \Head{} for the first time at most
once, and a plan cannot be non-empty without containing such a package. The
bound is restricted to the invariant domain. Publishing a suppressed
source or changing channel admission may expose a finite follow-up obligation;
\spec{19.6} requires re-planning and renewed review, not an unconditional
global-convergence claim. Held packages remain excluded.

\begin{proposition}[when publishing anything is progress]
\label{prop:progress}
Assume the retry invariant, (R) and $\Kprop \subseteq \Kblock$. Then every
well-formed run that publishes at least one package makes progress. Under
these hypotheses \cref{thm:g6} therefore holds with its hypothesis weakened
to ``every run whose plan is non-empty publishes at least one package''.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let run $j$ publish a non-empty set, and let $p$ be the first published
package in the publish order. Suppose $\Head \in \relc{\st_j}{p}$. By
\cref{lem:tagged-target}, $p$'s only causes are units $u$ with
$\admit{\st_j}{u}{p}$, and every $P \in \owed{\st_j}{u}{p}$ is in
$\plan{\st_j}$, untagged at \Head{}, with $\dirb{\st_j}{P} \neq \bnone$, and
precedes $p$ in the order. No such $P$ was published, since $p$ is the first;
none was blocked, since a direct bump is a cause of its own; so every such $P$
failed and lies in $X_p$. Then $\owed{\st_j}{u}{p} \subseteq X_p$ for every
admitted $u$, and with the first three disjuncts of \cref{def:owncause} absent,
$\lnot\owncause{\st_j}{p}{X_p}$. By (R) some owed $P$ reaches $p$ over
\Kprop{}, hence over \Kblock{}, so $p$ transitively depends on a member of
$X_p$ and is blocked: a contradiction with its being published. Hence $\Head
\notin \relc{\st_j}{p}$ and the run makes progress.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a non-blocking propagation edge]
\label{rem:optional}
Under the default configuration \Kprop{} contains
\code{optionalDependencies} and \Kblock{} does not, so (K) fails and
\cref{prop:progress} does not apply. The weaker hypothesis then does not
suffice. Let $p$ optionally depend on $P$, let one commit carry
\code{feat(P)\^{}}, and let $P$ fail on every run. In run 1, $p$ is not
blocked, since the edge is not in \Kblock{}, and it has no cause of its own;
it proceeds anyway, is reconciled to $P$'s old baseline, and is tagged at
\Head{}. In run 2 it is owed $P$'s release (\cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:tagged}),
is planned after $P$, and, $P$ failing again, is published again with nothing
new. Every run publishes a package; no run makes progress; the plan never
empties. The count $n$ of \cref{thm:g6} bounds the runs that make progress,
not the runs that publish, and a spec-level reading of ``successful-progress
run'' must say so. The republished $p$ carries nothing its predecessor lacked,
which is a second cost of the same configuration. Both are flagged for the
specification. The reference implementation does not exhibit the scenario:
it blocks over every declared dependency kind and treats an update from a
failed provider as no cause (\cref{ob:kinds}), so the caught-up consumer of
vector 80d is planned again each run and blocked with \diag{W194} until its
provider publishes, each such run exiting non-zero; the remark concerns the
specification's default kinds, not the engine.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[universal tagging is necessary, \spec{13.10a}]
\label{prop:tagall}
Assume the retry invariant. Suppose the protocol were weakened so that a released package $p$ whose publish
target uploads no artefact is versioned and has its manifest written, but is
not tagged. Then if $\effb{\st_1}{p} \neq \bnone$, $p \in \plan{\st_j}$ for
every $j$, and \cref{thm:g6} fails.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$p$ gains no tag in any run, so $\Head \notin \relc{\st_j}{p}$ for all $j$; by
\cref{lem:window} $\Winf{\st_j}{p} = \Winf{\st_1}{p}$, and by
\cref{lem:deliv}\ref{dl:unrel} $\owed{\st_j}{u}{p} = \owed{\st_1}{u}{p}$ for
every unit. Hence $\dirb{\st_j}{p} = \dirb{\st_1}{p}$ and, every clause of
\cref{def:admission} being constant, $\propb{\st_j}{p} = \propb{\st_1}{p}$.
The plan's first disjunct holds at every $\st_j$, so $\mu_j \geq 1$ for all $j$
in the proof of \cref{thm:g6} and no run empties the plan.
\end{proof}

This is why \spec{13.10a} tags whatever is released, including private,
internal and artefact-less packages, and why the convergence check of
\spec{19.6} (\diag{E199}) admits exactly one exclusion, held packages. A
permanent exception to convergence would not be a caveat to document; it would
disable the one check that detects the orphan failure of \spec{13.7a}, and
disable it precisely for the internal-only packages where nobody is watching a
registry.

\subsection{G7: channel discharge}

\begin{theorem}[\G{7}, channel discharge]
\label{thm:g7}
Suppose $d$ is released in run $k$ on channel $ch$, so that
$\chanof{\base{\st_j}{d}} = ch$ for $j > k$. Then for every unit $u$ and every
$j > k$, $\mathrm{pcf}_{\st_j}(u,d) \neq ch$: the channel $d$ has arrived on is
never proposed for it again.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $cur = \chanof{\base{\st_j}{d}} = ch$; this is read from $d$'s tag, not
from anything computed in the run. Every branch of \cref{def:propchan} ends in
the test ``if $target = cur$, return \NONE''. A result of $ch$ would require
$target = ch = cur$, which that test rejects. The transition branch fails one
step earlier where it applies: having arrived at $t$, $d$ no longer matches an
$f \neq t$, and for $f = *$ with $t = \stable$ the match requires
$cur \neq \stable$, which fails.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[channel-axis ledger discharge]
\label{cor:g7}
After any release of $d$, $\Winf{}$ excludes every delivered channel
directive (\cref{lem:window}), and \cref{def:phase1} admits none of them. The
no-op and transition checks of \cref{thm:g7} independently prevent
re-proposing the channel already recorded in the baseline. These are
complementary checks; the baseline comparison is not a substitute for
fresh-work admission.
\end{corollary}

The two axes discharge differently. The channel axis discharges through the
target's undelivered window, with the no-op check on top; the bump axis
discharges through delivery (\cref{thm:g4}), which on a target that has not
released past the commit is the same window and on one that has is stricter.
Under the retry invariant both survive a partial failure unchanged. This also
marks the boundary of what \cref{thm:g7} gives. It says the channel $d$
arrived on is not re-proposed; it does \emph{not} say nothing is proposed. The
historical CCME 1.0.0 counterexample in \cref{ob:pre} used its unsplit window
to let a second, older directive propose a different channel on every run. The
fresh-window gate, normative since CCME 2.0.0, discharges that directive after
release.

\subsection{G8: axis independence}

\begin{theorem}[\G{8}, axis independence]
\label{thm:g8}
The channel assignment $\chanres{\st}{\cdot}$ is a function of \st{}, \Head{}
and the configuration that does not read any quantity computed by phase 3.
Consequently, altering the bump directives $\pbump{u}, \pdepth{u},
\pscope{u}$ of any set of units leaves $\chanres{\st}{\cdot}$ unchanged, and in
particular a \diag{W208} suppression removes no channel assignment and causes
no package to lose a channel change it would otherwise have had.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{def:phase1}, phase 1 reads the units' channel directives, $\srcp{\st}{u}$,
the traversal, $\Winf{\st}{\cdot}$, $\canc{\cdot}{\cdot}$, and, through
\cref{def:propchan}, $\base{\st}{\cdot}$. By \cref{def:channels}, phase 2
reads phase 1's output and the same fixed quantities. None of these is a
phase-3 output: $\propb{\st}{\cdot}$, $\effb{\st}{\cdot}$, the admission
predicate and $\owed{\st}{\cdot}{\cdot}$ appear nowhere in
\cref{def:propchan,def:phase1,def:channels}. So
$\chanres{\st}{\cdot}$ is determined before phase 3 runs and is invariant under
any change confined to phase-3 inputs.
\end{proof}

The dependency between the axes is thus a single directed edge, from channels
to bumps, and \cref{thm:g8} is the statement that there is no edge back. Two
operational consequences follow, both worth stating because they are what a
plan reader needs: a \diag{W208} in a plan means a caret did not reach, not
that a channel directive failed; and a package may legitimately appear in a
plan with a channel change and no bump (\diag{W202}), or with a bump and no
channel change, and neither shape indicates a defect.

\begin{remark}[the converse does not hold]
\label{rem:g8-converse}
CCME 1.0.0 stated \G{8} as ``suppressing a bump under \spec{9.3a} never
suppresses a channel, \emph{and vice versa}''. \SPEC{} states only the
one-way result above. The old proof observed that
the passes share only $\chanres{\st}{\cdot}$, which phase 3 reads and never writes.
That argument establishes exactly the direction proved above. The converse is
false, and intentionally so: a channel suppressed by \diag{W200} leaves
$\chanres{\st}{d}$ at its baseline value, and a bump from sources on another
channel is then suppressed by \cref{a:resolvable}. \Cref{ob:g8} states the
correction.
\end{remark}
