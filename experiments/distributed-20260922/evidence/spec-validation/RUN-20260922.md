## What the outputs support

`mindiam.py` reproduces the validation session's statement: centre-joining forest completion equals the brute-force minimum-diameter optimum on 1,500 random forests; name-order Kruskal is worse on 461 of them with a maximum gap of 3; the "two 5-chains" line is the withdrawn topology example (Kruskal 9 hops, centre-join 5 and 5).

`relation.py` reproduces the session's statement: the chain, fan-out and fan-in makespan formulas agree with the longest path on random instances; the unlimited-slot makespan is monotone in the relation; the greedy schedule never exceeds `L + Wb/mb + Wp/mp`; the worst greedy-to-lower-bound ratio seen is 1.727; 146 of 4,000 random DAGs under slot budgets show Graham's anomaly, `independent` slower than `output`; the smallest is three packages with build/publish durations (2,3), (1,2), (8,3), package 1 consuming package 0, two build slots and one publish slot, makespan 11 under `output` against 12 under `independent`; the worked example of the specification gives 31, 18, 17 and 17.

`proceed.py` does **not** print the figures its author quoted in the handover ("4000 random histories: current window admission orphans the proceeding consumer in 247 histories, the delivery rule in 0, about 1.3% more releases"). The copied script runs 3,000 trials and prints 354 orphaned histories under the old rule and 194 under the new rule, with 236 extra releases. The author's own caveat was that the script "parses all units and so missed the §13.3 window-visibility gap the implementation found later"; the copied version evidently models a different state of the rule than the one the quoted figures came from, and no earlier version was retained. Neither set of figures is cited by the technical report; the quoted figures must not be cited from this copy. The definitive statement of the delivery rule is the specification text at commit 252216ba and its integration tests.

## Follow-up: the two rerun scripts retained by their author

After the note above reached the validation session, it retained the inline reruns that had produced its quoted figures as two scripts importing the originals, and stated that `proceed.py`'s own orphan check was over-strict (it counted a consumer as orphaned whenever it declared anything but the provider's newest release, whereas propagation does not cascade, SPEC §9.2, so a consumer is owed only the first provider release carrying a unit that propagated to it); the 354/194 printed by the plain `proceed.py` are that check's artefact. Both scripts were copied beside the originals and rerun by the research session from byte-identical copies; outputs verbatim.
